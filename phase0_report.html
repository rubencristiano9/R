A systematic ~60-minute gap between Forex Factory and Investing.com, 2023 onward

Root cause confirmed and corrected: Iran abolished daylight saving time in September 2022 and has held a fixed +03:30 offset since (from the Sep 21/22 midnight transition onward — Sep 21 daytime was still +04:30), but Forex Factory's file keeps alternating +03:30 / +04:30 seasonally all the way to the end of its coverage (April 2025) — the scraper behind it is still simulating a seasonal shift that stopped happening in reality. Fix applied: for every row dated 2022-09-22 or later, the Iran-local wall-clock time is reconstructed with a fixed +03:30 offset instead of trusting the file's own (phantom) suffix. Combined with the D2011-13 loading fix (which surfaced more comparable occurrences from 2011–2013, some of which disagree by a few minutes — real-world scheduled-vs-actual variance, not a data bug), DISAGREE across the full corrected run is 31. These are not uniformly small — most are single-digit minutes, but several are large (Fed Chair Powell Speaks −1050/+180/+45 min, President Trump Speaks −300/−90 min, FOMC Member Bullard Speaks −150 min, two CPI y/y readings at exactly −60 min) — see the full table below, which is the actual candidate set for manual verification once you approve moving to that step.

Reading the full NFP and ISM Manufacturing PMI series (not just a sample) instead shows Investing A is cleanly fixed-UTC−5 for essentially its entire range (2011 through mid-Feb 2018, and Oct 2018 onward) with one sharply bounded anomaly: 2018-02-15 through 2018-09-07 exactly, per the raw conflict data's own min/max — earlier versions of this report said "March–September" from spot-checking only NFP/ISM, whose next occurrence after Feb 15 happened to fall in March, understating the window — where multiple unrelated categories (NFP, ISM, Fed Rate Decision, Housing Starts, Jobless Claims...) are consistently ~60 minutes off from the established pattern, then snap back to normal in October — a real, narrow data-quality problem in that one file, in that one window.
